# RECIST 1.1: rules for the reader

Sources:
- E09: Eisenhauer et al., EJC 45:228 (2009)
- S16: Schwartz et al., EJC 62:132 (2016)
- FAQ: the EORTC RECIST working-group FAQ (recist.eortc.org)

## Measurability (E09 §3.1)

- **Measurable non-nodal lesion:**
  - CT long axis ≥ 10 mm with slices ≤ 5 mm;
  - with thicker slices, ≥ 2× the slice thickness.
- **Lymph nodes,** measured by short axis in the axial plane:
  - ≥ 15 mm: measurable;
  - 10 to < 15 mm: pathological, followed as a non-target;
  - < 10 mm: normal, not recorded.
- **Non-measurable:**
  - small lesions;
  - leptomeningeal disease, ascites, pleural or pericardial effusion;
  - inflammatory breast disease, lymphangitic involvement of skin or lung;
  - abdominal masses or organomegaly not confirmed by reproducible imaging.
- **Bone:** lytic or mixed lytic-blastic lesions are measurable only through their soft-tissue component (≥ 10 mm). Blastic lesions are non-measurable.
- **Cysts:**
  - Simple cysts are not malignant lesions.
  - Cystic metastases may be measurable, but non-cystic lesions are preferred as targets.
- **Previously treated lesions:** a lesion in an irradiated or otherwise locally treated field is usually not measurable unless it has clearly progressed.

## Target lesions (E09 §4.2)

- **How many:**
  - Up to **5** in total and **2 per organ**, representative of all involved organs.
  - FAQ: "lung", "kidney", "ovaries", "lymph nodes" each count as **one organ**, whatever the number of parts.
  - S16: lymph nodes are one organ, so at most 2 target nodes per patient.
- **How to choose:**
  - Choose by size (longest diameter) **and** suitability for **reproducible repeated measurement**.
  - If the largest lesion is not reproducible, take the next largest that is.
- **Sum of diameters:** long axes of non-nodal targets plus short axes of nodal targets.
- **Organ definitions that the guideline leaves open:** liver = 1 organ, peritoneum = 1, bone = 1 by convention. Pleural-based lung lesions count as lung.

## Non-target lesions (E09 §4.2)

- All other lesions, including pathological nodes of 10–15 mm, are recorded as present, absent or unequivocal progression.
- They can be grouped, e.g. "multiple liver metastases".

## Follow-up (E09 §4.3)

- Measure the same lesions, in the same way.
- A lesion too small to measure gets a default of 5 mm; a lesion that has disappeared gets 0 mm.
- Nodes: record the actual short axis even when it is < 10 mm, so the sum may not reach 0 at CR.
- A fragmented lesion: add up the long axes of its parts.
- Coalesced lesions: measure the combined lesion. The other lesions count as 0.

## Response of target lesions

| Category | Criterion |
|---|---|
| CR | all non-nodal targets disappeared, and all target nodes < 10 mm short axis |
| PR | sum ≥ 30 % below baseline |
| PD | sum ≥ 20 % above the nadir (smallest sum so far, baseline included) **and** ≥ 5 mm absolute |
| SD | neither PR nor PD |

## Non-target response

| Category | Criterion |
|---|---|
| CR | all non-target lesions gone, and all nodes < 10 mm |
| PD | unequivocal progression of existing non-target disease, so substantial that the overall tumour burden clearly increased, even when the targets are SD or PR. This is rare |
| NonCR/NonPD | everything else |

## New lesions (E09 §4.3.5, S16)

- An **unequivocal** new lesion means PD. It must not be explained by different scanning technique or by something other than tumour (abscess, inflammation, flare, healing bone). This matters most when the baseline lesions respond: necrosis of a liver lesion can look like a "new" cystic lesion, which it is not (E09).
- There is no minimum size, but small size is the typical reason a new finding is equivocal (E09).
- Equivocal: keep following; confirmation by a subsequent exam should be contemplated (S16). If the next scans show it is definitely a new lesion (e.g. it has not resolved, or has significantly increased in size), PD is dated to the scan where it first appeared.
- Lesions that coalesce are measured together (S16), not counted as a new lesion.
- A lesion in an area not scanned at baseline is new.
- A node that was normal at baseline and is now pathological is new (S16).

## Why target selection matters

Readers choose different targets in 59 % of patients. Response agreement drops from κ 0.97 with the same targets to 0.58 with different targets (Kuhl 2019).

Choose consistently:
1. the largest lesions;
2. covering the involved organs;
3. reproducible;
4. clearly separate from neighbouring structures.
